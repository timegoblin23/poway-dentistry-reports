Poway Dentistry · Creative intelligence · Revised Invisalign video

The offer stops the scroll. The greeting spends it.

A 31-second doctor-to-camera film for the $2,000 off Invisalign offer. The banner earns the pause. The first words are "Hi, I'm Dr. Mina". The film's best tension line, "If Invisalign is not for you, I will tell you", arrives at 16.7s, by which point most viewers have already scrolled away. The structure is tighter than the old cuts. The order is backwards.

The answer

Is there a hook? Half of one. The persistent "GET $2000 OFF INVISALIGN" banner is a real scroll-stopper because the price is the account's strongest lever. But it is static text. The spoken and visual opening is a credential greeting, and that is the most skippable second-and-a-half in dental advertising.

Is there a hold? Structurally yes, emotionally no. At 31s with a cut every ~2.4s after the first shot, it is built to retain better than the 49–110s cuts it replaces. But the first shot runs 6.6 seconds without a cut, and the middle tells the viewer about the clinic instead of giving them a problem to stay for. On this account, hold is what separates a $3.20 click from a $4.21 click.

The fix is mostly a re-order, not a reshoot. Open on "honest advice — if Invisalign isn't for you, I'll tell you", move the credentials to proof, and say the $2,000 out loud before the CTA.

Hook audit
Partial stop
  • ✓Offer on screen from frame one. "$2,000 off Invisalign" is the account's real lever. The winning static (Image ads 3) is offer-led too.
  • ✓A real face, sound-off ready. Doctor-to-camera with burned-in captions works for local trust.
  • ✗No pattern interrupt. A static medium-wide shot of someone standing in an office looks like every dental ad in the feed.
  • ✗The first words are a greeting. "Hi, I'm Dr. Mina" asks the viewer to care before giving them a reason.

Expected hook: about the account's 22–25%. The opener is the same doctor-to-camera format as the Model Video cuts, so there is no reason to expect it to stop more people. The banner is doing the stopping, and it was already doing that.

04 · Get the hold

3–15 seconds: retention mechanics present, a reason to stay absent

Hold is measured at ThruPlay (15s). Everything the viewer needs to keep watching has to happen before then.

What should help hold
Built to retain
  • Length. 31s against 49.5s, 51s and 110s predecessors. The full film is roughly two ThruPlays long, not seven.
  • Cut rhythm after 6.6s. Nine changes in 22 seconds: A-roll / B-roll alternation gives the eye something new every ~2.4s.
  • Captions every 2–3 words. High-contrast boxed captions pull sound-off viewers through the sentence.
  • A single voice. One person, one through-line, no music-only gaps. The audio never drops out (no silence over 0.4s).
What will leak hold
No open loop before 15s
  • The 6.6s opening take. The longest shot sits in the window where hold is decided.
  • Seconds 3–13 are a clinic tour. Years in practice, the team, the process. All true, none of it a problem the viewer owns.
  • The tension lands after the gate. "Honest advice… if it's not for you, I'll tell you" is the only open loop, and it closes at 19.3s.
  • Stock B-roll breaks trust. The open-mouth and black-glove shots don't match the clinic or the line being spoken.

Expected hold: above Model Video 10.0's 10.7%, likely in the 16–22% range given the length and cut rhythm. To reach Model Video 10.0 v2's 25.4%, the viewer needs a reason to stay before second 5, not just faster cuts.

06 · Keep and change

What the cut gets right, and what to change before it spends

Keep

  1. The persistent offer banner. Price is this account's proven lever. Image ads 3 carried 7 of 9 form leads on the same offer.
  2. Dr Mina on camera. A real, local, named doctor beats the stock-model look of the old cuts.
  3. "Honest advice. If Invisalign is not for you, I will tell you." The best line in the film, and the most distinctive. No competitor will say it.
  4. 31-second length and the captions. Both point at hold.
  5. The 3D-scan and mirror-smile shots. One is proof of technology, the other proof of outcome.

Change

  1. Open on the honest-advice line, not the greeting. Move "Hi, I'm Dr. Mina, 20 years…" to seconds 6–10 as proof.
  2. Cut inside the first 2 seconds. No shot before 0:05 should run longer than ~2s.
  3. Say the $2,000. Banner and voice should land together once, right before the CTA.
  4. Replace the open-mouth and black-glove stock. Stay on Dr Mina for "I will tell you": that line needs eye contact.
  5. Match the end card to the voice. "Free consult" or "free video consult": pick one, and trim the 0.5s black tail.
  6. Deliver 9:16 and 4:5 masters. 3:4 will be cropped or letterboxed on Reels and Stories.

Same audience, offer and copy in both arms. Hold up, CPC flat means the story is holding attention but not creating a reason to click. CPC down means the tension landed.

09 · Method and limits

How this was read, and what it cannot say

Video. "Revised Poway Dentistry.mov", 31.5s, 2160×2880 (3:4), 60fps. Frames sampled at 2fps; shot boundaries from ffmpeg scene detection (threshold 0.25), checked by eye; one wipe at ~13.3s added by hand.

Audio. Transcribed with Whisper large-v3-turbo; timestamps ±0.3s. Captions read off the frames. No silence longer than 0.4s at −35 dB before the end card.

Baseline. Meta Ads API, ad-level, lifetime (18 Sep – 9 Nov 2026), USD. Hook = 3-second video views ÷ impressions. Hold = ThruPlays ÷ 3-second views. ThruPlay is 15s for all four assets, so they are comparable.

Small sample. Four video assets and $1,068 of spend. The hold–CPC link is consistent but directional. Audience, campaign objective and ad set differ between instances.

Predictions are hypotheses. This cut has no delivery yet. The expected hook and hold ranges are reasoned from format and structure, not measured. Replace them with the first 2,000 impressions.

The lens explains the narrative. It does not override the offer, the landing page, or the form. The website path converts 2–3% of landing-page views, so a better video moves CPC first and CPL only as far as the page allows.
